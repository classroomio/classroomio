Sales Discovery Call Playbook

A discovery call has one job: find out whether there's a real problem worth solving, and whether you're the right people to solve it. Everything below serves that job.

Before the call

  • Read their website and one recent post from the person you're meeting.
  • Write down the one assumption you most want to test.
  • Pick three questions from the bank. Not ten.

The first five minutes

Confirm the time, share the agenda and ask permission to take notes. Then ask what made them take the call today, and stop talking.

# Product 101 course builder

## Goal
Draft a Product 101 course that gets a brand-new customer to their first
successful outcome with the product.

## Before drafting, ask
1. The product name and the one sentence you use to describe it.
2. The three jobs customers hire the product to do.
3. A link to the help center or docs site.
4. Who the learner is: admin, end user, or both.

## Structure
Create six sections, in this order:
1. Why it exists (one lesson, under 3 minutes)
2. First win in 10 minutes
3. Core workflow, one lesson per job from question 2
4. Working with your team
5. Getting help
6. Final check (10 questions, multiple choice, auto-graded)

## Style
- Lessons are 400–700 words. One idea per lesson.
- End every lesson with a single action the learner does in the product.
- Use the customer's product vocabulary from the help center, not generic terms.
- Never invent features. If the docs don't cover something, ask.
