# How top customer academies shape their courses

Supporting research for the [course builder plan](README.md). We checked 11 software academies on 18 November 2026 from their live pages.

**How we picked the "top 5" courses:** few academies publish popularity, so each table names its signal:

- **Canva:** learner counts.
- **HubSpot:** one course labelled "Popular".
- **Everyone else:** "start here", featured or path placement.

"n/s" means the page doesn't say.

**Two academies dropped:**
- **Shopify Academy:** now redirects to the Shopify blog.
- **Google Skillshop:** its catalog needs a login.

## Patterns

**Titles** are short (2–7 words), outcome-led, and name the product. Four formulas recur:

1. **Imperative outcome:** "Get started quickly with Jira", "Set up your team in Webflow".
2. **Product for a role:** "Webflow for Marketers".
3. **Topic + Essentials:** "Canva essentials", "Keyword Research Essentials with Semrush".
4. **Label: outcome:** "Welcome to HubSpot: What to Do in Your First Week", "Admin Essentials: Govern AI as your people build".

Feature names sit inside a job verb ("Track your work effectively in Jira"). Bare feature titles appear only at lesson level.

**Sequence a new customer sees:**

| Order | Course | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orientation: what the product is, how to get around, one first action | 13–30 min |
| 2 | Core workflow, end to end | 45–90 min |
| 3 | Role track, usually admin or team setup (users, roles, permissions, integrations). A parallel track in 9 of 11 academies | — |
| 4 | Advanced, automation or AI, or a certification | Certifications run 3–14 h |

**Size:**
- Getting-started courses have 3–7 lessons.
- Core courses have 7–24 lessons in about an hour, in 3–5 sections, opening with a welcome and closing with "next steps".
- Lesson videos run 1–10 minutes.

**Checks:**
- **A quiz at the end of each course is universal.** Size varies: Zapier uses 4 questions, Canva 19–25, monday.com 45 (80% to pass).
- **Many check after every module too:** HubSpot, Webflow's "Review & practice", Trailhead.
- **The strongest courses add hands-on work in the learner's own account.** Examples: HubSpot practicals, Trailhead challenges, Semrush set-up steps, Zapier's "write your automation as one sentence".

**Media:**
- Academies use their own short videos, usually presented by named staff, often with transcripts.
- Other common media: worksheets, templates, cheat sheets and cloneable projects.
- We saw no academy use embedded YouTube as its main lesson format.

**What makes them feel tailored:**

1. A named audience, and who it is *not* for.
2. Outcomes written as "you'll be able to…" in the product's own words.
3. Real work in the learner's own account (a first folder, a first user, a first Zap).
4. Exact click paths with a link to the help article.
5. Real customer stories and real builds.
6. Each course says what comes next.
7. Awareness of the customer's plan ("available on Pro").
8. Concept first, then application ("Understanding X / How to use X", "Strategy / Walkthrough").

## What this means for generated starter courses

Applied in the [course builder plan](README.md) §4:

- **Titles:** use the four formulas above, name real features, and keep to 7 words or fewer.
- **Course 1:** make it a short orientation (2 sections × 2–3 lessons, about 15 minutes) that ends in one first action. Courses 2–4 run 3 sections × 3 lessons.
- **Admin course:** make one course an admin or team track when the material shows team, roles or permissions features.
- **Descriptions:** every description says who the course is for, and lists 3 "you'll be able to…" outcomes.
- **Section openings:** open each section with the concept, then the walkthrough.
- **Course endings:** close each course with a short "next steps" lesson pointing to the next course.
- **Checks:** a short check after each section, plus one hands-on task per course done in the learner's own account (the importer's LINK or TEXTAREA question types). Example: "Create your first form and paste its share link."
- **Plan notes:** say so when the material ties a feature to a plan.
