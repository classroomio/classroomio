We make ClassroomIO, so we are on this list and we know it best. We tried to judge every platform on the same questions: what it costs on the public price list, what it is strongest at, and who should pick something else. Prices were checked on 15 November 2026 unless noted, and vendors change them, so confirm on their pricing pages before you buy.

If you want the definition first, read [what a customer academy is](/blog/what-is-a-customer-academy).

## How we compared them

- Is there a published price, and what is the first paid tier?
- Can you run a branded academy on your own domain?
- Does it have certificates and learner reporting?
- What is it clearly better at than the others?

## The short version

| Platform | Published starting price | Strongest at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ClassroomIO | Free for 20 students, then $35 a month | A low-cost branded academy with certificates and an AI tutor |
| Disco | $399 a month billed annually | Cohorts, live events and community |
| Trainn | $2,400 a year (Launch), LMS on Scale at $10,000 a year | Videos, guides and in-app tutorials |

| Teachfloor | $89 a month (Startup, checked October 2026) | Cohort learning with peer review |

| Intellum | Quote only | Enterprise programs with several audiences |

## ClassroomIO

ClassroomIO is built for teaching customers. The free plan covers 20 students. The $35 plan adds your own domain, branding, certificates, unlimited collaborators and up to 10,000 students. It also has an AI tutor inside lessons, AI course drafting, a public API, webhooks and MCP access, and the code is open source.

It is a weaker fit if you need native Salesforce or Gainsight connectors, which it does not have, or published SOC 2 documents for procurement. Read the full [customer academy software page](/customer-education).

## Disco

Disco is a learning and community platform. Its Create plan is $399 a month billed annually ($499 billed monthly) and includes 250 active members and one branded academy. Scale starts at $999 a month and adds SSO and branded mobile apps. If your academy is built around live events, chat and leaderboards, Disco is the stronger choice. If you mainly need to teach customers a product, the starting price is high. See [ClassroomIO as a Disco alternative](/alternatives/disco).

## Trainn

Trainn starts from content creation: screen recording, videos, step-by-step guides and in-app tutorials, with a knowledge hub on top. The LMS appears on the Scale plan at $10,000 a year with 100 monthly active learners. Launch is $2,400 a year for 3 users and 120 videos and guides. There is no free plan, only a 14-day trial. It lists SOC 2 Type II as verified. If most of your work is making videos and guides, it is a good fit. See [ClassroomIO as a Trainn alternative](/alternatives/trainn).

## Thinkific

Thinkific is built to sell courses and memberships to the public, with communities and payments. Its Basic plan was $54 a month when we checked in October, and the self-serve plans come with 1, 2 or 6 admin seats. It works for a customer academy, but you pay for a storefront you may not need. See [Thinkific alternatives](/alternatives/thinkific).

## Teachfloor

Teachfloor focuses on cohort-based learning with peer review. The Startup plan was $89 a month in October, with 50 seats and one automation. Branding and domain sit in the custom-quoted plan. See [Teachfloor alternatives](/alternatives/teachfloor).

## Skilljar

Skilljar is an established customer education platform with deep CRM integrations and proctored exams. It does not publish prices. It sells an annual subscription plus a fee based on active users, and third-party estimates start around $30,000 a year, which is indicative only. It suits large teams with sales-led buying. See [Skilljar alternatives](/alternatives/skilljar).

## Intellum

Intellum is a modular enterprise platform that quotes each product separately: LMS, authoring, AI agents, e-commerce, social and data connector. It makes sense when you serve several audiences with separate catalogs and sign-on. See [Intellum alternatives](/alternatives/intellum).

## How to choose

- If you are a small SaaS team starting out, pick the cheapest platform that gives you your own domain and certificates, and avoid a contract before you have a first course.
- If your academy is mainly a community with live events, look at Disco.
- If your bottleneck is making videos and guides, look at Trainn.
- If procurement requires SOC 2 documents, a CRM integration or a sales-led contract, look at Skilljar or Intellum.

Whichever you pick, launch one onboarding course first. The [30-day launch plan](/blog/customer-academy-launch-plan) shows how, and ClassroomIO's [free plan](/signup) is enough to build it.
