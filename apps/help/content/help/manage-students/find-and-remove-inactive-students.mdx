If your audience has grown faster than your usage, the recurring question is: **who isn't using the platform, and who should we remove?**

This guide walks that loop end to end. Filter, review, act, and keep a record of what happened.

## The short version

<Steps>
<Step>
### Filter to the dormant students

Open **Audience** and pick a saved view, or build your own filter.

</Step>

<Step>
### Export the list

Export it to CSV or Excel and circulate it for sign-off.

</Step>

<Step>
### Archive them in bulk

Select all matching, choose **Archive**, and add a reason.

</Step>

<Step>
### Check the result

Undo is available for 15 minutes if the list was not what you expected.

</Step>
</Steps>

## 1. Filter to the dormant students

The fastest start is a saved view:

| View                        | Finds                                                    |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Never logged in**         | Has an account or a sent invite, but has never signed in |
| **Inactive 90+ days**       | No course activity in 90 days                            |
| **Inactive 180+ days**      | No course activity in 180 days                           |
| **Enrolled, never started** | Has courses assigned but has not begun any               |

For anything more specific, combine filters, such as **No login in 180 days** with **Completion, not started**. See [Filters](/reference/audience#filters) for the full set.

:::note[Recent joiners are excluded automatically]
Any time filter switches on **Exclude recent joiners**, so a new intake does not appear in these views. The grace period is the filter's own window, and 7 days for **Never logged in**.
:::

Two columns tell you whether "inactive" is the whole story. **Last Login** is when they last signed in; **Last Activity** is when they last did something in a course. Someone logging in without any activity is a different problem from someone who has vanished. The first may be stuck rather than disengaged.

## 2. Review before you act

Export the filtered list and circulate it. This is the step that makes the rest safe, because it turns a bulk deletion into a decision somebody signed off.

Use **Export as CSV** above the table, or the dropdown for Excel. The file follows your filter exactly, so what you send is what you will act on. See [Exports](/reference/exports).

## 3. Archive, deactivate, or delete

Once the list is agreed, tick the students you want, or use **Select all matching** to take everyone the filter returns.

**Archive is almost always the right action.**

|             | Archive   | Deactivate | Delete  |
| ----------- | --------- | ---------- | ------- |
| Access      | Removed   | Removed    | Removed |
| Record kept | Yes       | Yes        | No      |
| Reversible  | Yes       | Yes        | **No**  |
| Plan seat   | **Freed** | Kept       | Freed   |

- **Archive** when you want the seat back. It is fully reversible, so it costs nothing to be wrong.
- **Deactivate** to suspend someone you expect to return, such as a member of staff on leave.
- **Delete** only when a record must not remain. It requires the student to be archived first.

Add a **reason** when you confirm. It is stored against every student in the run, so six months later the decision is answerable.

Deleting removes the student from your organization and from every course in it, including their course completion records. Their ClassroomIO account and lesson progress remain, so the work they did is not erased.

## 4. Check the result, and undo if needed

The confirmation tells you how many students changed and how many were skipped, usually because they were already in that state.

**Undo** then stays available for 15 minutes after an archive or a deactivate, and applies to exactly the students who changed rather than to a re-run of the filter. If someone else has since acted on one of them, their newer decision is kept.

The Undo button lives on the page. Leaving or reloading before you use it clears the offer, though the change itself is easy to reverse by hand: filter to the state you applied and run the opposite action.

Runs of more than 1,000 students continue in the background so you can keep working. Keep the tab open to see the result, because the notification arrives on this page.

## Getting the seats back

Archiving is what reclaims capacity: an archived student does not count towards your plan's audience limit. Deactivating does not free a seat, which is the main reason to prefer archive when the goal is headroom.

If you archive people and the limit banner does not clear, check that you archived rather than deactivated. The [Audience reference](/reference/audience#membership-status) sets out the difference.

## If you get it wrong

- **Within 15 minutes**, and still on the page: use **Undo**.
- **Later**: filter to **Status, Archived**, select the students, and choose **Unarchive**. They return exactly as they were, with their enrolments and results intact.
- **After a delete**: this cannot be undone. The person can be invited again, but they return as a new member of your organization, without their previous enrolments.
