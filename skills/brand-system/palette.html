ClassroomIO — colour foundation

One warm ramp. One blue.

Every marketing asset sits on Bone. Changelog videos, social cuts, academy materials, carousels and the site all draw from this one ramp, so they look related without being identical.

Blue is the colour people should remember — it belongs in every asset, at a size you can actually see. What it must not be is the background, or sprayed across every element at once. Bone is the ground that makes it register.

Rules

Blue should be obvious, not ambient

Every asset needs a blue element big enough to register from across a room — a filled chip, the key word in the headline, the live state, the CTA. If someone has to hunt for the blue, there is not enough of it.

Blue is a mark, not a ground

It goes on the things that carry meaning: the active item, the number that matters, the action — not on every badge, bar, icon and rule in the same frame. .field-blue is the deliberate exception, and it spends the accent: on a blue field the accent resolves to white, so nothing can be blue to stand out.

State colour is a dot, not a scheme

Green #1D6B40 and amber #A6620E exist for done and needs-attention. One dot or one number — never a badge, a bar and a tick for the same fact.

Grain on every field

A fine noise overlay is most of why flat colour reads as designed rather than default. It lives on the stage and costs nothing.

Across the assets

Weekly changelog videos

Bone throughout, one Ink frame for the cover or the sign-off. The version number is a filled blue chip, and each shipped item takes the blue as it lands.

Social video

Ink fields cut through a bright feed better than Bone. Use data-tone="dark" for the hook frame, Bone for the body.

Academy materials

Bone as the page, Paper for cards and worksheets, Bone 300 to separate sections. Blue only on the action a learner should take.

Marketing carousels

Alternate Bone and Bone 300 slide to slide so the deck breathes. Cover on Ink, and every slide carries one blue element — the number, the label, or the CTA.
